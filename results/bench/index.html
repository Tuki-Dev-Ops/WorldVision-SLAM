Descriptor-free vs. classical: a side-by-side SLAM benchmark

Left is the classical descriptor pipeline. Right is WME. Same frames, same undistortion, intrinsics, depth scale and keyframe rule; scored by a third program that estimated neither. The per-sequence panels are odometry only; loop closure is measured separately, further down, with a back-end shared by both systems.

Two controls, and neither is ORB-SLAM3. ORB + PnP — Hamming descriptor matching (Lowe ratio 0.75) → RANSAC PnP → LM refinement, 1000 features, 8 pyramid levels, ORB-SLAM3's own front-end settings — but with no loop closure, local map or bundle adjustment. cv2.Odometry — OpenCV 5's published dense RGB-D odometry, written by nobody here, run through the identical data path; it is the evidence that the hand-written control is not under-tuned. Every ATE below is measured against the better of the two, because one control flatters: against ORB alone the tally is 12–4.

Verdict

Absolute trajectory error, all sequences

Lower is better. The dashed rule is the “camera never moved” floor, recomputed per sequence — anything at or above it has learned nothing.

Per-frame error over time

Frame-to-frame relative translation error against ground truth — the quantity neither system optimises directly.

Under degradation

Haze applied to the real frames by the scattering equation I·t + A·(1−t), with t = exp(−β·d) evaluated on TUM's measured depth — so the transmission is not an approximation of scattering. Measured contrast tracks the prediction to within 2%.

Same keyframes, same candidate proposal, same pose graph, same robust kernel. The only difference is how a proposed loop is verified.
